# Capiro

Capiro is a cross-platform desktop app for capturing, annotating, and exporting polished showcase assets.

It sits between a plain screenshot/capture tool and a full video editor. Its job is to turn raw screen captures into clean, postable showcase material with as little manual editing as possible.

## What it is

- A capture app with a real point of view
- A modern markup and annotation workflow
- A light editing path for showcase polish
- A free and open source desktop app
- Built with Wails v3

## What it is not

- Not a full video editor
- Not a full After Effects replacement
- Not an attempt to clone every CleanShot X feature on day one
- Not a generic capture utility without a curated workflow
- Not macOS-only

## First workflow

The first workflow Capiro is built around is simple:

1. Capture a screen area
2. Annotate or mark it up
3. Export a polished shot with strong defaults

That loop should work immediately with minimal configuration.

## Why it exists

The existing options are fragmented.

- CleanShot X is compelling but macOS-only.
- ShareX is available on Windows, but the experience can feel dated.
- Flameshot covers basic capture and markup well, but does not fully reach a polished showcase workflow.
- Snagit is feature-rich but enterprise-heavy and expensive.
- shots.so-style treatments are useful for static showcase imagery, but limited when you want more than a static frame.

On top of that, the workflow Capiro targets often still involves manual polishing in After Effects: mouse motion, framing, spacing, and turning captures into a showcase-ready result.

Capiro exists to reduce that manual work and give people a single, modern place to capture, mark up, and produce showcase assets without forcing them into a heavier or more expensive stack.
